Muster your force

Solitaire and co-operative play: your commando — or two of them, one each — against an OpFor that acts in a phase of its own, driven by the behaviour table. Commandos are small: build yours from the commando composition table. The OpFor pool is rolled for you from its own table, and in a co-op game it is rolled a Priority Level higher for the second player.

A mercenary company, an insurgent group, a bug swarm or a Xenotripod tribe fights one of the book's six scenarios on a 4′ × 4′ table. Pick your force, a Battle Tier and a Priority Level, then spend composition points — the sum of your units' Tiers — against the rulebook's table.

How this plays

A faithful digital skirmish built on the PMC 2670 rulebook by Marcin Gerkowicz (Assault Publishing). The view is isometric, but the rules run on a flat 4′ × 4′ table: movement, range and line of sight are all measured in inches, edge to edge between the two closest models, as you would with a tape.

Mustering a company

  • All 40 PMC infantry profiles are in, exactly as printed — Basic troops, Rifle infantry, Assault troops, Heavy infantry, Light infantry, Light and Heavy support, Remote mortars, Command units, EW and Medic teams, and the Unclassified Nomads and Chem-warriors.
  • All 36 PMC vehicles and aircraft too — Combat vehicles, Hunters and Destroyers, Transports, Engineering, Support, Anti-aircraft, EW and Medical vehicles, Transport aircraft and Strike aircraft.
  • Battle Tier I–V and Priority Level 1–2. Composition points are the sum of your units' Tiers — 6, 12, 18, 24 or 30, multiplied by the Priority Level — and the per-Tier minimums and maximums come straight from the book's composition table. Tier III is the book's recommended starting point.
  • The limits are enforced as you build: at least three units of the Battle Tier, at most four a Tier below, two a Tier above, one Command Unit per Priority Level, Penal troops four to an army, High command one, EW and Medic teams one per Priority Level, Chem-warriors two.
  • Machines have their own ceiling: at most three ground vehicles and aircraft per Priority Level, aircraft only from Battle Tier II, and at PL1 no machine above the Battle Tier and only one aircraft. A Gunboat, a Light engineering vehicle and an EW vehicle are one to an army each, and an Anti-aircraft vehicle one per Priority Level.
  • Every ground vehicle picks a propulsion from the optional rules — wheeled, tracked, anti-grav, hover or walker. None of them change a Unit Tier, because each trades an advantage for a cost; pick one from the dropdown beside the unit in your list. Every ready-made company fields at least one machine.
  • Roll a legal company at a button, or start from a ready-made one — two per Battle Tier.

The battlefield

  • Terrain. Movement penalty: area terrain costs 1″ (2″ for a vehicle) once in a move, however much of it is crossed, and a unit starting in it pays to move through it; each low wall crossed costs it again. Barbed wire costs a D6″ rolled before the move. Hills block sight across them; a unit on one gets +2 Firepower shooting down and sees over its own side below. A big hill may rise in two steps: its crown is a hill on the hill, blocking sight across it for everyone but a unit up on it, and a unit on the crown gets the +2 shooting down on the slope below as well as on the ground — +2 once, however many steps down. A low wall shelters only a unit with every model within 2″ of it, the wall between it and the shooter; rubble shelters the men in it and no one behind it. Trenches give cover and immunity to Crossfire. House rule: Protectors in hi-mobility armour jump over terrain — no penalties, and over walls, water and buildings — but must land on ground they could stand on.
  • Buildings. Enter building and Exit building are special actions, within 4″; one unit to a building (to a section, in a building of several wings, and Next section moves through to an empty one in contact). Inside: no Move, Advance or Assault; +2 Defence; no Crossfire; range measured from the wall both ways. A high building or a reinforced one gives +2 Firepower, a low one none — high buildings are drawn a storey taller. Charge a garrison and win, and you take the building while the defenders come out and fall back 2″. A building can also be garrisoned by tapping it during deployment.
  • Terrain set-up by hand. Choose Set it up by hand and the game walks the table one 2′ × 2′ area at a time, starting from a random player and alternating: it rolls the area's D6, you pick between the options a result offers (hills or rocks), and every piece the result gives is shown drawn, together, the next one outlined; turn the next one a quarter at a time (Turn it, R, or a right click on the table), then tap each piece down — anywhere in that area, up to the number rolled. The OpFor lays its own areas. Either area, or everything left, can be auto-placed. In Meeting engagement, Secure and control and Find and secure the table edges are rolled only after the terrain is down, as the book has it, and the table is turned to match. Campaign battles use whichever set-up you last picked here.
  • Terrain generators. Pick a planet — or randomise it — and the table is built the book's way: divided into 2′ × 2′ areas, with a D6 rolled for each and the rolled result placed in it. Every roll and what it produced is listed in the log and on the opening card.
  • Where a result offers a choice ("1-4 hills or rocks") one branch is taken at random, and counts like "1-6 rocks" are rolled within their range. Small rocky areas count as rubble, as the book says; high rocks, deep canyons and volcanoes are rocks, and swamps are shallow water.
  • Terrain effects follow the summary table: woods and ruins give +2 Defence and block sight; rubble and low walls give +2; buildings give +2 Defence and +2 Firepower; reinforced buildings +4 Defence; hills give +2 Firepower shooting down; rocks, high walls, deep water and lava are impassable; shallow water costs movement and silences Cumbersome Weapons.
  • Natural shapes. Woods, rubble, rocks, water, lava and hills are laid down as irregular outlines — round patches, lobed clusters, kidney shapes and long diagonal bands — rather than rectangles, and every effect stops at the drawn edge: a unit standing where the ground is open is in the open, a sight line that only grazes the empty corner of a wood's footprint is clear, impassable ground is only as big as it looks, and a hill's +2 Firepower is only on its top. Buildings, walls and ruins stay square. The open ground is coloured to suit what stands on it — greener by woods and water, dry by rock and buildings, baked round lava — but that is only paint: open ground is open ground.

The six scenarios

  • Every scenario is in, and the D6 that picks one is the book's. At Tier I–II or Priority Level 1 the roll may be a D3 instead, taking one of the first three, exactly as the book allows. In a campaign the roll is made on the contract screen; in a one-off you can also choose.
  • Meeting engagement — no objectives at all. The only way to win is to rout the other company: destroy or drive off half its units. Twenty turns.
  • Secure and control — three objectives, at least 12″ apart and 8″ in from the edges. Hold more of them at the end of turn 20, hold all three at any End phase, or hold the same two for three End phases running.
  • Find and secure — three possible locations within 12″ of the centre, and nobody knows which. A unit within 4″ uses the Check the area! action: the first location gives the prize up on a 5+, the second on a 4+, and the third is where it was all along. Hold it for three turns running to win outright — and a company holding it cannot be routed while it holds it. Half each force starts in reserve, and from turn 3 a number equal to the Priority Level comes on every second turn. After turn 12 a D6 is rolled each turn: a 6 ends it, and one point easier every turn after that.
  • Invasion — three landing zones. The attacker's whole force comes down from orbit rather than deploying: the first wave lands in the Reserve phase of turn 1, and infantry take D3 Suppression on the way down — vehicles are unaffected. The second wave rolls in from turn 4 on a 5+, easier every turn. The defender puts no more than a third on the table and rolls the rest on from turn 2 on a 5+. Battlefield Insertion is switched off for both sides. The attacker wins by holding two of the three zones at the end, or by routing the defender.
  • Demolish — the defender's objective sits within 4″ of the table centre, impassable, and the ground around it is cleared. It comes down only to the Demolish action, and this is the one piece any attacking infantry may go for — Sappers charge it at +4, everybody else at +2. Half the defenders deploy within 18″ of it and the rest roll on from turn 2. The objective's SAM system opens up automatically on any aircraft that finishes its move within 12″, at Basic Firepower 12. Twelve turns.
  • Hostile takeover — one objective at the centre of a prepared position. The defender digs in first: up to ten sections of wall, none longer than 6″, and a single bunker, all within 12″. The defender deploys within 12″ of the objective; the attacker splits in two, the first part entering on turn 1 and the second from turn 3. The attacker has to hold the objective at the End phase of turn 20.
  • Attacker and defender are rolled for in the three scenarios that have them, and The Best Defence is Good Offence (a Strategic doctrine) lets a company that is made the defender roll a D6 and take the attack instead on a 2–6 — unless the other company has the same doctrine, in which case it does nothing.
  • An interpretation: the book has most scenarios entering the whole force “from your table edge in the Reserve phase of turn 1”, which is the same thing as deploying in a strip on that edge, so that is how it is presented. The scenarios with a genuine deployment area — the defender in Demolish and Hostile takeover, and the defender in Invasion — get one.

The campaign

  • Press Campaign in the header, or “Play a campaign instead” at the bottom of the muster sheet. Found a company — six Tier I units, two Tier II, a free Tier I Field command, at most two vehicles, and one of the eighteen doctrines — then take contracts.
  • Solo or hotseat. Solo puts you against a rival PMC with a character of its own: Armoured, Elite, Swarm, Marksmen or Shock. It founds in its own style, reaches for its own doctrines, recruits into its own unit groups and spends experience its own way, so it grows into something recognisable over a dozen battles. You can read its dossier between contracts.
  • Each contract rolls the Battle Tier on a D6 capped at the weaker company's Company Tier, rolls the scenario, and has you pick your force from the roster rather than the catalogue.
  • Units have a history. They keep a name you give them, earn experience for turning up, for winning, for fighting a bigger company and for breaking an enemy — and they collect Trauma Points for being broken, for losses, for defeats and for fighting battle after battle. At ten points (fifteen with Mental Training) comes a Battle Trauma; at ten Traumas the unit is disbanded.
  • Spending — promotion keeps the unit's identity, honours and traumas and only changes the profile; a Battle Honour draws three and takes one of the three at random; vehicles and aircraft take Upgrades instead. Then recruitment, and the Company Tier itself.
  • Everything is saved in the browser, and mirrored to your account when the page can reach it, so a campaign follows you between devices. You can also export it to a file and load it back.
  • On the table: Rapid Relocation gives you a step after deployment to pick up to half your units and set them down again; NOT ONE STEP BACKWARDS! adds a special action to a Command Unit and anyone within 12" of one — shoot at a suppressed friend: the shot is resolved as normal, so a Man down! still kills, but every Suppression point it would give comes off instead of going on; Semper Fidelis lets a unit held in a scenario reserve come on any turn after the first, no roll — you are offered it each Reserve phase and may keep it back, and its transport comes with it if it is the only unit aboard; Bloodlust leaves a unit only one choice when an enemy is in charge reach: charge the closest, Cumbersome Weapon or not; Nanobots repair on dice for the whole Structure.

Space Bugs

  • The swarm is the third army on the muster sheet. Lesser, Underground, Spore, Flying and Overgrown bugs, led by a Leader Bug: Watcher larvae at Tier I up to the Queen at Tier V. Bugs have no vehicles to buy, no Drones and no Upgrades — the Overgrown bugs fight with vehicle profiles and facings instead.
  • Overmind — a Leader Bug controls every friendly bug of its own Tier or lower within 18" (24" with Increased Control). Selecting one draws that reach as a violet dashed ring. Bugs under control lose every Suppression point in the Rally phase, take cover from terrain and ignore Aggressive.
  • Animal Behaviour — a bug out of the Overmind's reach takes no Defence from cover. Aggressive bugs out of reach must charge the closest enemy they can.
  • Endless Tide — in the End phase, an unbroken swarm within reach of an unsuppressed Overmind gets D3 lost bugs back, up to its starting size.
  • Pheromone Markers give Animal Behaviour bugs +1 to hit (up to +3) for each marker unit within 18" of the target. Psychic Wave puts D6−1 Suppression on every enemy within 12", no line of sight needed; Drones are immune.
  • Flying bugs ignore the ground but take no cover and may not stop on impassable terrain. The Carrier bug is an aircraft with Transport (4). The Huge underground bugs arrive by Battlefield Insertion.
  • The swarm campaign spends Resource Points rather than money. The free Leader Bug grows with the Swarm Tier; any extra Leader Bugs must sit below it. Leader Bugs earn no experience and take no trauma. Tiny bug swarms spawn free while you hold four or fewer.
  • Pathways replace doctrines — eighteen in three branches (Biochemical, Behavioural and Phenotypic), from Effective Toxin Glands and Chitin Exoskeletons to Metal-covered Talons and Increased Control. Adaptations and Flaws replace Battle Honours and Traumas; Atavism strips a unit's Adaptations again.
  • Feeding — with Alternate Carbon-based Metabolism every model killed in an assault pays RP, and with Fungi Symbiosis every human unit destroyed seeds a free unit of Infected. A bug that dies stays dead: there is no salvage.
  • Rivals — a solo campaign may throw a swarm at you instead of another PMC, in one of four characters: Ivenbea, Evatus, Terarson or Hydra.

Xenotripods

  • The tribe is the fourth army on the muster sheet. Crocks lead and specialise — Alpha commanders, Beta recon in deflector armour, Gamma heavy weapons, Delta blades — and the Esh-Aven are the Epsilon line troops, nine to a squad. There are no ground vehicles: the tribe flies triangular craft and teleports turrets in. At most three turrets or turret sets and aircraft a Priority Level, and one Alpha squad a Priority Level.
  • Limited Senses — a Xenotripod sees only 12". Mental Projection — but whatever one unbroken Xenotripod sees, the whole tribe sees: a unit may fire at anything in range that the tribe can see, as long as nothing blocks its own line, and units with Indirect Fire shoot over the terrain without needing a Markerlight. Selecting a Xenotripod draws every other member's 12" of sight and rings each enemy the tribe can see. Turrets are drones: normal sight, and no part of either rule.
  • Psychic Bond — a unit rallies on the Morale of any friend within 6". Each model the tribe loses puts a Suppression point on every friend within 6".
  • Cloaking System — shot at or charged only from 12" or less. Shield Generator — friends wholly within 12" get +2 Defence (+1 from a craft) against shots from outside it; the best one counts. Psychic Support counts as Field Medics.
  • Special actions — Regain Control (Dominant Species): an Alpha stands still and every Epsilon squad of its Tier or lower within 12" sheds all its Suppression. Self-repair (Molecular Reconstruction): a turret or craft stands still and clears all its Damage. Teleport: a pad takes in an infantry unit within 4"; on a D6 of 1–2 it comes out at a random Teleport unit, on 3–6 at the one you choose, and it can still act that turn.
  • Turrets — stationary, with no sides or rear. They are Drone Controlled, but a hacker's 5–6 only counts as 3–4. All of a side's turrets act together as one activation, and they don't count towards Overwhelming Numbers. A turret set is one pick, but every turret in it is a unit on the table, counted as Tier I.
  • The tribe campaign is paid in Territorial Points. In Invasion, Demolish and Hostile takeover the payment is recalculated: a winning tribe counts dice of 1–2 as 3 (1–3 as 4 with Effective Resource Utilisation), and a losing one counts 5–6 as 4. The aftermath shows the dice before and after.
  • Who costs what. The free Alpha squad grows with the Tribe Tier; any extra Alphas sit below it. Alphas and turrets never earn experience or trauma. Primitive Epsilons are free to raise while you hold four or fewer, and free to promote. Turrets are never bought — they only take composition points.
  • Tribe Advancements replace doctrines — eighteen in three groups (Social, Organisational and Technological), no more than two from a group. Some act before the battle: Foresighted Command rolls a second scenario to choose from, Detailed Terrain Knowledge moves two terrain pieces, Fortify and Strike! throws up four low walls, Complex Teleport Network brings a free set of Teleport turrets. Enhanced Genetic Memory regrows a lost infantry unit, and on 2–6 it remembers everything it had.
  • Rites and Infamies replace Battle Honours and Traumas (a D20 and a D10 table). Aircraft are never promoted and take no Rites: at 10 EXP they take one of ten aircraft upgrades instead, from Emergency Batteries to the Vortex Generator.
  • Rivals — a solo campaign may send a tribe against you: Mithdu-2 (slow, turrets and tunnels), Amt (Gammas and craft), Ghadon II 3rd (aggressive), or Hashamer I, an Esh-Aven tribe heavy on Epsilons and Deltas.

The maths is the real thing

  • Shooting — D10 + Firepower + model-count bonus (+1 at 3–4, +2 at 5–6, +3 at 7+), +1 for a stationary Fire!, +2 inside half range, +2 for Crossfire, +2 firing from a hill. Every point over the target's modified Defence is one hit. Unmodified 0 always fails; unmodified 9 always succeeds for at least one hit.
  • Hit table — D6 per hit: 1 Steady boys, 2–5 Get down (1 SP), 6+ Man down (1 model, 2 SP). +1 against a Broken unit, +1 more when the attack is a Crossfire.
  • Assault — defensive fire at Basic Firepower first; a charge that leaves the attacker Suppressed or Broken fails on the spot. Then three rounds by the attacker, three by the defender, with 2–3 Ouch and 4–6 Man down. A break at any point ends it with a 2" fall-back; if nobody breaks, the attacker falls back.
  • Suppression — over Morale is Suppressed, over twice Morale is Broken, over three times after a rally attempt the unit is removed. Morale drops by one per model lost, minimum 1, unless the unit is Determined.
  • Rally — D6 equal to current Morale, each 4+ clears 1 SP. Inspiring Presence re-rolls failures within 12". The phase is walked unit by unit: the camera goes to each one and its card shows every die before you continue.
  • Every special rule in the book's General list is in. Advanced Protection, Anti-aircraft, Anti-tank, Anti-tank (limited) — which only reaches 6" — Battle Armour with the second Defence value that Anti-tank and Gauss weapons strip away, Battlefield Insertion, Command Unit (X), Command Vehicle, Counter-jamming, Cumbersome Weapon, Determined, Expendable, Field Medics, Gauss Weapon, Hackers, Incendiary Ammunition, Indirect Fire, Inspiring Presence, Jammers, Keen-Eyed, Limited Fire Arc, Markerlights, Minimum Range, Sappers, Specialisation (air) and (ground), Stealth, Supporting Fire and Suppressive Fire — plus Drone Control, Transport (X), Auxiliary Weapons, Crossfire and Overwhelming Numbers.
  • Jammers put every enemy within 24" onto 5+ to rally, and their vehicles onto 5+ to repair; Counter-jamming within 6" of the victim cancels it. Field Medics within 6" turn the hit table into 1–2 steady, 3–5 a suppression point and 6 a MEDIC! — which costs a single point, and a further D6 of 6 saves the casualty outright.
  • Terrain comes down. Low walls, high walls and ordinary buildings are destructible; reinforced walls, bunkers, woods, ruins and rocks are not. A unit with a Destructive Weapon can shell a piece down with the Demolish action, and Sappers can charge one with Breach — either way a final 15+, or an unmodified 9, brings it down, and a failed charge falls the sappers back 2". Incendiary Ammunition counts as a Destructive Weapon against buildings, and a Tier III–V hull simply drives through a low or high wall and flattens it.
  • Firing through cover — when a Destructive Weapon shoots at an enemy sheltering in or behind a destructible piece and rolls a final 15+ or an unmodified 9, the piece comes apart in the same attack: the target loses its Defence bonus and the hits land one step harder. Sappers do the same in the first round of an assault, with a +4 for the charges.
  • What is left — a flattened wall becomes rubble that shelters nobody and can be walked over; a wrecked building burns, blocking line of sight and barring the ground, and its garrison scrambles into the open at once. Supporting Fire lets a carrier shoot — without the stationary +1 — and still load or unload afterwards.
  • Drone Control is a build option on any ground vehicle without Transport: one more Structure for having no crew, at the price of being reachable by Hackers. A hacker within 24" rolls a D6 — 1–2 nothing, 3–4 the drone is locked out for the turn and takes D3+1 hits, 5–6 it is first turned on its own side.
  • Battlefield Insertion — LRRP teams, sniper teams and Nomads are held in reserve rather than deployed, up to half the company, and come in during the Reserve phase of any turn but the first. Nominate a point at least 12" from an objective and 4" in from the table edge; on a D6 of 1–3 it holds, on 4–6 the opponent shoves it up to 2D6" — towards their own guns. Coming down inside 12" of the enemy draws an immediate Basic Firepower shot from the nearest gun that can see them, which costs that unit nothing.
  • Vehicles — Structure instead of Morale, and Damage points instead of Suppression. Every hit is rolled on the armour table: 1 bounces, 2–5 is one Damage, 6+ is D3 — D6 if the shot came from an Anti-tank or Anti-aircraft weapon. Vehicles add +4" on a Move, pay a turn cost in inches off that distance, pay 2" for difficult ground, never take cover from terrain and are never caught in a Crossfire. They are hit harder from the flanks: +1 to the firing roll from the side, +2 from the rear. A Limited Fire Arc bears only on the front quarter. Vehicles never charge, and infantry assaulting one get +4 and roll on the breach table.
  • Knocked out — when Damage passes Structure, D6: 1–3 Abandoned, 4–5 on fire (the passengers take a Basic Firepower 6 attack), 6+ a catastrophic explosion that kills everyone aboard and hits every ground unit within 4" at Firepower 3 + the vehicle's Tier. More than 2 Damage past the kill adds +1 to that roll, more than 4 adds +2. In the Rally phase a machine repairs itself: a D6 for every remaining point of Structure, each 4+ clearing 1 Damage — 5+ while an enemy's Jammers are within 24".
  • Propulsions — wheeled is the standard. Tracked cuts Movement to three quarters but takes 1" off the turn cost, which on most hulls means turning for free. Anti-grav adds a quarter to Movement at the price of one Structure point, and still pays for difficult ground. Hover crosses water and other liquids — never lava — but pays 1" more to turn. A walker pays the infantry 1" for difficult ground instead of 2", takes Defence bonuses from terrain like a footed unit, and its armoured flanks deny the +1 side shot, but its height costs it 1 Defence.
  • Turning — a vehicle's turn cost comes off its move, so the ground inside its front quarter is cheaper to reach than the ground behind it. The shaded move area shows the difference.
  • Transport (X) — a carrier loads infantry within 4" and may then move half its distance. Passengers come off the table, lose all their Suppression and cannot be shot at; a Broken or Suppressed unit cannot board, and nothing unloaded may re-board the same turn.
  • Aircraft — they fly above the table, always shoot and are always shot at with Basic Firepower, never take cover, are never caught in a Crossfire, and are destroyed outright the moment Damage passes Structure. They cannot assault, cannot be assaulted and cannot hold objectives. A Strafing Run flies the full move and fires at every enemy ground unit passed over; the survivors shoot back, and a friendly unit under the flight path is caught on a D6 of 1–3.
  • Specialisation — a SAM team's or an Anti-aircraft vehicle's Specialisation (air) means its main weapon cannot engage ground troops at all, exactly as written, and Specialisation (ground) is the mirror of that.

Reading the table

  • Move shades every point the unit can actually reach — a true distance field that bends around rocks and costs an extra 1" to enter difficult ground.
  • Auras — selecting a unit draws its weapon range, its half-range bonus band, and the ground it can actually see, traced against the terrain.
  • The camera — the table is bigger than the window, so drag or scroll to move it, + and − to zoom, F for the whole table. It follows whichever unit is acting.
  • On a touch screen — tap a unit to select it, tap an action, then tap the target or the shaded ground. Drag to pan, pinch to zoom. A unit off the edge of the screen can be tapped in the force list instead, which brings the camera to it.
  • Watching it happen — units walk their actual route, bending around terrain; shots throw tracers and kick up dust where they land; assaults strike sparks; a wiped-out unit fades from the table. The resolution card waits until the shooting stops.
  • Sound is synthesised in the browser — rifle cracks, rounds striking home, boots, dice. Toggle it from the header.
  • Measuring — hovering with a unit selected drops a tape to the cursor. Ranges are edge to edge between tokens.
  • Checking ground — tap any open ground and a note says what the terrain does there, how far it is from the selected unit, whether it is in range, and what the move would cost.
  • Cover — +2 Defence for standing in cover, and +2 for low cover sitting on the line between the two units.
  • Odds before you commit — pick Fire!, Auxiliary or Assault and every ringed target carries the exact chance of the attack telling, with the average number of hits beside it. It is worked out by walking all ten faces of the die against the modified Defence, honouring the unmodified 0 that always fails and the 9 that always scores — the same sum the roll itself uses, so the number never flatters the shot.
  • Every roll opens a resolution card with the full arithmetic, and lands in the combat log afterwards. Cards stay up until you press Continue (or Enter, or Escape) — tick "Advance OpFor cards automatically" on the card if you would rather the enemy's results flick past on their own.

Interpretations

  • A unit is a single token rather than a spread of models, so coherency, base-to-base contact and per-model casualty placement are abstracted.
  • Markerlights mark a target for the turn instead of chaining an immediate friendly activation.
  • Broken units fall back 4" toward their own edge in the End phase — the rulebook says a broken unit tries to flee without giving a distance.
  • Both forces deploy inside a 6" strip; the Reserve phase is used only by Battlefield Insertion, not for scenario reserves.
  • Terrain is placed at random inside each rolled area rather than positioned by the players, and the objectives are kept clear of it.
  • Buildings are entered by standing in them; one-unit-per-building, the entry action and crossfire immunity are not modelled.
  • Objectives are held by an unsuppressed unit within 4" with no enemy within 4", per Secure and Control.
  • The 4" blast from a wrecked vehicle is read as following the catastrophic explosion result only, not every destruction result.
  • A vehicle's turn cost is charged once, against any move that leaves its front quarter, rather than per 90° turn along the route; vehicles do not reverse at half speed.
  • Tracked Movement is kept exact — three quarters of 10" is 7.5", not 7" or 8" — because the table is measured in real inches.
  • A Rider's mount — bike, grav bike or horse — is chosen on the muster sheet and changes the model only; the profile is the unit's own.
  • A high wall comes down as a whole piece rather than in 6" sections; the generator is capped so that it never lays one longer than 6" in the first place.
  • A Command Unit riding in a Command Vehicle lends the hull all its rules, and coordinates the moment the hull finishes its activation — the book lets the player choose whether to, which here happens automatically.
  • Rapid insertion platforms, turrets, barb wire, and trenches are not in this version.
